# 14 Paid App Ideas — Real Personal Problems With a Reason to Keep Paying

Built by the **idea making** team in `#App Ideas`: Nova (body & mind), Rio (money & work), Sage (people), Kai (daily life), Chase (digital life & skills + merge).

---

## The bar we set

The ask wasn't "give me app ideas." It was: *what problem is so real that someone pays every month to keep the app?* So before anyone wrote a word, we agreed on one filter and applied it ruthlessly:

> **What happens on the day they cancel?**
> If the answer is "nothing — they keep their data and go back to normal," the idea is dead.

That single question kills most consumer app ideas, including a lot of popular ones. A budgeting app, a habit tracker, a home inventory, a meditation app — you get the value, you plateau, you cancel. They're one-time-value tools wearing a subscription costume.

What survived has at least two of these five engines:

| # | Engine | What it means | Example in this doc |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **External recurring trigger** | Someone *other than the user* restarts the loop — an employer, an illness, a school year, a statement cycle | Nightshift (a new roster drops every 2–6 weeks for a 20-year career) |
| 2 | **Compounding archive** | The data gets more valuable every month and cannot be rebuilt from memory | Casefile, Vest, Keystone |
| 3 | **Deadline with teeth** | Missing a date costs real money — appeal windows, tax quarters, warranty expiry | Recourse, Float, Unspent |
| 4 | **Dollars out > dollars in, visibly** | The app shows you the money it recovered, so renewal is arithmetic, not a judgment call | Unspent, Snout, Recourse |
| 5 | **Multi-person lock-in** | Other people are inside the account, so it survives any one person losing interest | Relay, Quorum, Snout |

The strongest ideas here run on a calendar **the world controls**, not one the app has to manufacture. As Kai put it in the thread: *the app doesn't have to create the recurrence, only be present for it.*

## How to read each idea

Every idea below has: the problem (who hurts, how often, what they do today), why it must be an app, **the honest paywall logic**, brainstorm notes including what we rejected and what we're unsure about, MVP + V2 features, real pricing with reasoning, a marketing plan for the first 1,000 users, and the biggest risk.

The brainstorm and risk sections are deliberately unflattering. Every teammate was told to name the assumption that would kill their own idea. Where a paywall is weak, it says so.
