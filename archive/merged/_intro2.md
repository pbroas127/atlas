# 12 Mass-Market App Ideas

### Huge audiences, sharp entry points, and a real reason to keep paying

Built by the **idea making** team in `#App Ideas`: Nova (the body), Rio (the money), Sage (the people), Kai (the stuff), Chase (brief + merge).

*Round 1 produced 14 sharply vertical ideas — shift nurses, IEP parents, equity-comp holders. Those are kept in full as [Appendix: the vertical bets](#appendix--the-vertical-bets). This section is the mass-market set.*

## The two gates

The ask was apps **anyone could use and need, where finding users isn't hard.** Those two things fight each other, and the whole document is built around that fight.

A universal audience sounds like easy marketing and is the opposite. "Everyone" has no subreddit, no conference, and no shared vocabulary. You're competing with free defaults already installed on the phone, and your positioning has to be bland enough to fit everyone, which means it grabs no one. Round 1's ideas were easy to reach *because* they were narrow.

So every idea here has to clear two gates at once:

> **CEILING** — why 50M+ people eventually need this.
> **WEDGE** — the one findable group of 100k–2M you reach in month one, where they already gather, and the path from them outward.

Facebook launched at one college. Venmo launched on roommates splitting rent. Uber launched on SF tech workers. Enormous ceiling, one specific first user.

Big ceiling with no wedge is a pitch deck. A great wedge with a small ceiling is Round 1. Both were rejected.

## The third gate: you are now competing with free

This is the gate that killed the most ideas, and it's the one that separates a mass-market app from a mass-market fantasy. At this scale there is always a free alternative already on the user's phone — the bank app, Apple Photos, the group chat, the payroll portal, YouTube, a chatbot.

So each idea has to name its free competitor and explain why it loses **structurally** — not "ours is nicer," but *the free option cannot do this because of what it is.* The patterns that survived:

| The free tool | What it structurally can't do |
|---|---|
| Your sleep tracker | Grades a night that already happened. It has discarded the afternoon, where the problem actually lives. |
| ChatGPT / recipe sites | No memory of your household. Optimized for discovery; the real problem is re-service. |
| Your payroll portal | Displays your stub perfectly and will never check it — your employer paid for it. |
| Your bank app | Sees the charge, not the contract. The money is in the contract. |
| Splitwise | Tracks debts *between* people, not proportion between incomes. Makes partners feel like roommates. |
| Shared calendar / to-do list | It's empty. Knowing what's coming is the actual labor. |
| Apple Photos / Files | Storage, not retrieval. It doesn't know the rectangle in image 3,412 is a passport expiring in March. |
| Retailer emails, the Shop app | Single-retailer, and none of them track return deadlines — because breakage is revenue. |

**The strongest position in this whole document:** where the party best placed to build the feature is the party that *profits from you not having it.* Your employer's payroll vendor will never flag your underpaid overtime. The retailer will never remind you the return window closes. That misalignment is permanent — it's not a feature gap someone closes in a sprint.

And the paywall test from Round 1 still applies, unchanged: **what happens on the day they cancel?** If the answer is "nothing," it's dead. Mass-market does not mean ad-supported.

## The 12

| # | App | Lane | What it does | Wedge — the first 1,000 | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dusk** | Body | Tells you what to do at 4pm so you don't sleep badly tonight | Sleep-tracker owners frustrated their ring never tells them what to *do* | $69/yr |
| 2 | **Rotation** | Body | Answers "what's for dinner" from what your household actually eats | GLP-1 users hunting 120g of protein on 1,100 calories | $59/yr |
| 3 | **Groundwork** | Body | The maintenance program that remembers what fixed your back last time | Recreational runners with recurring injuries | $89/yr |
| 4 | **Stub** | Money | Checks your paycheck, because nobody else does | Hourly shift workers with overtime and differentials | $39/yr |
| 5 | **Uptick** | Money | Catches every silent annual increase across your household contracts | Homeowners who just opened a brutal insurance renewal | $59/yr |
| 6 | **Pool** | Money | The money system for two people who share a life but not a paycheck | Couples moving in together for the first time | $69/yr |
| 7 | **Kinkeep** | People | Remembers the people you love so you're not the one who forgot | Newlyweds facing 120 thank-you notes | $59/yr |
| 8 | **Even** | People | Tells your household what's coming before one person has to notice it | First-time parents in the 0–12 month window | $79/yr |
| 9 | **Keepsake** | People | A weekly question to the people you'll lose, kept in their own voice | New grandparents | $99/yr |
| 10 | **Glovebox** | Stuff | Your car's permanent record — and proof of it when you sell | Out-of-warranty used-car owners on model forums | $39/yr |
| 11 | **Drawer** | Stuff | Every household document, and a warning before anything expires | New parents in the eight-week document burst | $49/yr |
| 12 | **Doorstep** | Stuff | Every package coming, every return unsent, every refund that never arrived | People who buy clothes online and return half of it | $39/yr |
